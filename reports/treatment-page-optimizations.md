```
**Is physical therapy effective for TMJ/TMD?**
```

```
## Frequently Asked Questions — Dry Needling

**Is dry needling the same as acupuncture?**
No. Dry needling is based on modern musculoskeletal anatomy and targets myofascial trigger points to reduce muscle tension and pain. Acupuncture follows traditional Chinese medicine meridian pathways. The needle looks similar but the approach, training, and goals are different.

**Does dry needling hurt?**
The needle itself is very thin (thinner than a hypodermic needle) and insertion typically causes little to no sensation. When the needle reaches a trigger point, you may feel a brief muscle twitch or deep ache — this is the therapeutic response and is a sign the treatment is working. Most patients tolerate it very well.

**How quickly does dry needling work?**
Many patients notice a reduction in muscle tension and improved range of motion within 24–48 hours of their first session. Full benefit often builds over 2–4 sessions.

**Is dry needling safe?**
Yes, when performed by a certified clinician. Dr. Julie Roy holds a dry needling certification and follows strict sterile technique. Single-use, disposable needles are used at every session.
```

```
## Frequently Asked Questions — Neurologic PT

**What is LSVT BIG?**
LSVT BIG (Lee Silverman Voice Treatment BIG) is a physical therapy protocol specifically designed for Parkinson's disease, backed by over 35 randomized controlled trials. **Is it too late to start physical therapy after a stroke?**
No. While early intervention produces the fastest gains, meaningful improvement is possible at any stage post-stroke. The brain retains neuroplasticity — the ability to rewire and adapt — throughout life. Consistent, task-specific therapy is the key driver of recovery regardless of when it begins.
```
